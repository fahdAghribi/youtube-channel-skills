# Research playbook

## Picking seed channels

- **Direct competitors** (same topic, same viewer): 2-4
- **Adjacent** (same viewer, different topic; e.g. a freelancer channel for an SEO channel): 2-3
- **Bigger, same topic, other language**: 1-2 (cross-language gaps)
- **Smaller channels with outliers**: 1-2. Outliers on small channels are the cleanest demand signal because there is no subscriber base inflating views.

Avoid channels with mostly Shorts when researching long-form (use `--no-shorts`).

## Reading outliers correctly

- Ignore videos younger than ~14 days (ratio not settled) and very old ones (> 2-3 years) unless evergreen.
- A ratio driven by one external spike (news, a viral tweet) is weak evidence: look for the same idea succeeding on 2+ channels.
- Note the format alongside the topic. Sometimes the format is the outlier ("I tested..."), not the topic.

## Gap patterns

1. **Outdated winner**: a 3+ year old outlier on a topic that changed (new version, new law, new price).
2. **Language gap**: proven in English, missing or weak in the channel's language.
3. **Level gap**: everyone does beginner, nobody does intermediate (or the reverse).
4. **Format gap**: all tutorials, no test/story/case-study on the same topic.
5. **Angle gap**: everyone sells the upside, nobody shows the cost or the failure.
6. **Comment demand**: recurring questions in competitors' comments that no video answers.

## Idea extraction template

> **Desire:** [what the viewer wants]
> **Proof:** [video] on [channel], [ratio]x, [age]
> **Our angle:** [how this channel does it differently, with which receipt]
> **Format:** [tutorial / test / story / list / comparison / case study]
